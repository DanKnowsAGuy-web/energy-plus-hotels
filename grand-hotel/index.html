Why your last upgrade didn't pay off.

~30%

According to the EPA, the average commercial building wastes about a third of the energy it consumes. Most hotels have never had anyone qualified find out where.

You added lighting. Maybe controls, maybe solar. Each vendor optimized the one thing they sell, then left. But a hotel isn't a stack of separate upgrades. It's one electrical system, and when pieces get bolted on without anyone designing how they work together, they interfere. Power quality degrades, equipment runs harder and fails sooner, and the savings on the brochure quietly leak away.

That interference isn't bad luck. Every vendor in this industry gets paid to install their box, so each one optimizes their own piece and none of them owns the result. Your building wasn't left incoherent by accident. It was left incoherent by incentive.

What a real diagnosis finds.

Single-box vendors never look at most of this. We measure all of it.

  1. Power quality

    A building running at a low power factor is billed for current that never does useful work, and harmonic distortion adds electrical noise that wears equipment out early. We measure both at the panel, before and after.

    Measured at the panel

  2. Demand and penalties

    Your electricity bill is energy charges plus demand charges plus any power-factor penalty. Much of the recoverable money lives in the demand and penalty portions, where most vendors never look.

    Energy + demand + penalty

  3. Systems that fight each other

    Your HVAC, lighting, and any on-site generation were almost certainly specified in isolation. We look at how they interact, because that interaction is where the waste hides.

    Whole-system interaction

None of this appears on a bill as a line item called waste. That is exactly why it survives for years.
